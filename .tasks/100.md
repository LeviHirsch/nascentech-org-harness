Levi's direction (10/2): a client kit that makes login one terminal command, like his own ssh nt (T140). What's left is how a person's laptop proves who they are to Cloudflare before SSH starts.

- Browser about once a month (Levi's way): cloudflared opens the browser by itself when the Cloudflare session ends. The session length is a setting, from 24 hours (the default) up to one month. Each connection is logged at Cloudflare under the person's email; removing someone means removing their email; and the stricter step chosen 8/12 (short-lived certificates) builds on this same sign-in. Fellows without a nascentech.com account can sign in with a one-time code sent to their email (a Cloudflare login option).
- Never: Levi creates a service token per person in the Cloudflare dashboard (an ID and a secret that last as long as he sets, for example a year). The kit keeps it in the person's Keychain and hands it to cloudflared. Removing someone means deleting their token. Costs: Levi issues and hands over one secret per person; Cloudflare logs show the token's name rather than an email; and the stricter step later would move people back to browser sign-in. cloudflared supports this (--service-token-id and --service-token-secret, or TUNNEL_SERVICE_TOKEN_ID and TUNNEL_SERVICE_TOKEN_SECRET; the Access policy action must be Service Auth), but it is unproven here until T137 runs.

Decide: which way for fellows (it can differ per person).

Tailscale stays the noted alternative (phones, or if Cloudflare is ever dropped), not the plan.

What it unblocks: the setup command (T138) and register-a-person (T103).

Intent: fellows get in with one command, with no more browser than Levi judges worth it.
