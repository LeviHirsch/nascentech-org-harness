Requirement (Levi): "Especially if mini has total control, how do we limit or guide permissions (less at first but more trust among users until we can implement more stringent measures) so that they can have as much control as possible without it being a problem in any way?" And: "At what point do we implement stricter security verification?"

Decided (Levi 10/2): trust the cohort now: house rules, every action stamped with the person, lock down later. Separation is a Lock-down item (T134), not a prerequisite.

What one shared account means: anyone signed in can use the service-account key (which can act as all 9 Google accounts), read everyone's files, and claim another name. So these guardrails guide and detect; they don't lock.

Layers:
- written rules (T112);
- the AI steered by rules it can't override (T113);
- tools that refuse (T6);
- a log of who did what (T11);
- alerts when the sign-in list is edited (T114);
- server sessions kept off Levi's machines (T101).

When stricter checks come in: when a trigger in T111 fires, Lock-down (T134) starts.

Open: T111 (the trust ladder and the triggers).

Done when: the rules are approved and visible, the AI follows them by default, the log attributes every sign-in and Google action, and edits to the sign-in list are reported.

Intent: people get as much control as possible without it being a problem.
