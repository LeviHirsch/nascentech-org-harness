Three programs now carry the name clv: Levi's personal launcher on his laptop (clv 0.1.0: list, add, show, rm, plus ~/.collevity/user/bin shortcuts), the server's identity tool on the mini (clv whoami, clv env, with clv-login and the Google tools beside it), and, from 10/3, the client kit's clv on fellows' laptops (clv setup, clv login, clv key, clv update; nt is its shortcut). Levi (10/3): "I'm all for the two existing programs becoming one codebase. Honestly, they're mixed up in my head so that would probably be very good to litigate."

Design question to settle in an interactive session with Levi, not by an agent alone: one codebase with subcommands that depend on where it runs (laptop, fellow laptop, server), or a dispatcher over separate parts; where the source lives (this repo, collevity-tooling, or the public tap); how one update reaches every machine; and how it carries the global and per-folder settings split (canon 56 and 81, T33).

Done when: Levi has ruled the shape, and one repo builds every machine's clv.

Intent: one tool, one name, one mental model, on every machine.

10/3: the kit's clv now lives in the public tap (LeviHirsch/homebrew-tap, folder clv/). Where clv's source should live long-term is part of this task. An interactive session, one-clv-design, was opened 10/3.

Thinking out loud, not a ruling (Levi 10/3, design session; his label: "it is thinking out loud"). Shape: "the basic core is the same between client and enterprise. More like just the specific content installed." "there is the core of the tooling which is for managing the directory basically as context AND how the USER interacts with it all at once. there's sort of a downward connection/bridge at any layer." Down: "the downward at a user install level is just the isolation of agents and such for context isolation." Up: "that also comes with all the plugin infrastructure for you to tie into your org/entity/server", and "the potential for having even more layers upward like departments and organizations". Live app wrapper, per folder or over the whole system: "both." Versioning, a later question: "most likely going to eventually have to be part of this… not a first degree thing though". Main target: "figuring out how to get all of this into something of a unified board", with products kept "separate enough but unified in their goal".

Ruled (Levi 10/3), on what "unified board" means: "B makes sense." B as put to him: each product keeps its own board and repo; one view shows every product's in-play tasks together under one goal line; a task is still picked up inside its product. The view does not exist yet.
