Registering someone is a by-hand edit today, with no record. Write it as a board flow ("register a person") and run it once per person:
- pick their name (the T99 rule);
- check the public key is a single ssh-ed25519 line and record its fingerprint;
- add the line binding the key to their name, with a backup;
- give them the way in (T100);
- link their Google account (T5);
- confirm they see their own name in clv whoami.

Each run's record is the access log until T87 records registration as an event. The flow also covers removing someone's access; do it once to prove it.

Done when: the flow exists, every current person has a run, and one removal has been done and checked.

Intent: "register a user on the server for the first time" is a known, recorded procedure, and so is taking access away.

10/2: the person's public key arrives from the kit's setup (T138). "Give them the way in" means adding their email to the Cloudflare policy, or issuing their service token, per T100.

Ruled 10/2 (T100): no service tokens, so the way in is the Cloudflare Google login. Removing someone means removing them from Google Workspace and deleting their key line in authorized_keys; the two gaps that leaves are T145.
